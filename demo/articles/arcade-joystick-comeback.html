The arcade joystick makes a comeback in small-batch cabinets

Builders in a few workshops say players want the click of a real stick and the weight of a real cabinet.

By Callum Reeve · November 6, 2026

In a workshop behind a bakery in the invented town of Brindle, a carpenter sands the side of a cabinet that will hold two joysticks and a screen. He has a waiting list of eleven months.

Small builders say the appeal is physical. A good arcade stick gives a satisfying click at every direction, and a button feels different when it is the size of a coin and sits under your palm.

Prices are not small. A finished cabinet costs about as much as a second-hand car, so many buyers choose a kit and finish it at home. Online forums are full of photographs of work in progress.

The builders hope the trend lasts. "People don't want a museum piece," said workshop owner Daniel Brandt. "They want something they can play every night."
