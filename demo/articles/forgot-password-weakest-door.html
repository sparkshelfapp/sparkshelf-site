Why "forgot password" is still the weakest door in your house

Attackers rarely break strong passwords. They simply ask the website to reset one.

By Noor Haddad · November 6, 2026

You can pick the longest password in the world and still lose an account through the small link beneath the login box. Security researchers call it the quiet door: the reset flow that lets anyone who controls your email become you.

In the invented case study "The Greywater Incident", the attacker never touched the victim's password. He took over an old, forgotten email address, pressed "forgot password" on a shopping account, and waited for the code to arrive.

Three small habits that close it

The advice is unglamorous. Protect the email address that receives your resets more than any other account, turn on a second step for it, and remove old addresses you no longer control.

Services can help too. Slower resets, a notice sent to every address on the account, and a short waiting period before big changes all make the quiet door harder to walk through.
