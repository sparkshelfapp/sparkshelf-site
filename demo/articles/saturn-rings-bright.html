Saturn's rings are brightest for amateur astronomers this month

Clear evenings and a favourable tilt make this a good time to see the planet with a small telescope.

By Elin Marchetti · November 5, 2026

This month Saturn rises soon after sunset and stays high for most of the evening. Its rings are tilted toward us at a gentle angle, which is enough to show the gap between the rings and the planet.

You do not need expensive equipment. A small telescope with a medium eyepiece shows the rings clearly, and a steady hand or a simple tripod does the rest.

How to see it

Give your eyes about fifteen minutes in the dark before you look. Start with a low magnification to find the planet, then step up slowly until the view starts to blur.

Look for a pale, sandy colour and a dark line in the rings, which observers call the Cassini Division. On a night with steady air, it appears as a thin, crisp gap.
