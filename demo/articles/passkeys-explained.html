Passkeys explained: what changes when there's no password to steal

A plain-language guide to signing in with your phone's unlock instead of a secret you have to remember.

By Samuel Okoye · November 4, 2026

A passkey replaces your password with a pair of keys. One stays on your device and never leaves it. The other is given to the website. When you sign in, your device proves it holds the first key, usually after you unlock it with a fingerprint or a PIN.

Because there is no shared secret, there is nothing for a fake website to capture. A phishing page can copy a login box, but it cannot make your device answer for the real site.

What if I lose my phone?

The practical questions are about loss. Most systems sync passkeys through your device account so that a new phone can restore them, and every serious service keeps a recovery route for when something goes wrong.

Our advice: add a passkey to your email and bank first, keep one recovery method up to date, and leave your old password in place until you are comfortable.
