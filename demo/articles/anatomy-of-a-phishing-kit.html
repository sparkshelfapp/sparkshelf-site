A guided tour of a phishing kit, and the five signs it leaves behind

We took apart a fake login page in a safe lab and found the same tells again and again.

By Noor Haddad · November 5, 2026

Phishing pages are built from kits: bundles of ready-made web files that copy a login screen and send what you type to someone else. In a locked-down lab, we opened one and looked inside.

The first tell is the address. The page lived on a name that looked like a bank but added a harmless-sounding word. The second is urgency: a countdown and a warning that your account would close in ten minutes.

The five tells

The third and fourth tells are subtler. The kit asks for more than a real login ever would, and it forwards you to the genuine site after you have typed, so that nothing seems wrong.

The fifth is the way you arrived. If a message pushed you to a login page, close it and open the site yourself. That single habit defeats almost every kit we have seen.
