Warehouse robots learn to ask for help instead of guessing

A pilot in a regional parcel depot shows fewer dropped boxes when machines call a human for the hard cases.

By Jonas Whitfield · November 5, 2026

The robots at the Eastfield parcel depot do not look like the machines from films. They are small, white and oddly polite, and since this summer they have learned an unusual skill: admitting when they are unsure.

When a robot meets a parcel it cannot grip with confidence, it now sends a short video clip to a person at a nearby desk, who taps the best way to pick it up. The robot remembers the answer for next time.

The depot says damaged parcels fell by almost a fifth during the trial, and the human helpers handle about forty requests an hour, far fewer than they expected.

Researchers at the invented Calder Institute say the approach is less about clever hardware and more about honest software. "A robot that knows what it does not know is much easier to trust," said Dr. Hana Lindqvist.

The depot plans to extend the pilot to night shifts and to share anonymised clips with other warehouses.
