A supercomputer hall opens its doors to small research teams

A national lab will rent unused hours to universities and start-ups, with a simple application form.

By Mara Ellison · November 5, 2026

The long, bright hall at the invented Marlowe National Laboratory holds more computing power than most countries had a decade ago. From next month, a slice of it will be available to anyone with a good idea and a short application.

Until now, time on the machine went mainly to large consortia. The new programme reserves one tenth of its hours for small teams: a thesis project, a start-up testing a model, a hospital looking for patterns in anonymised scans.

How to apply

Applicants fill in a single page describing what they want to compute and why. A rotating panel of researchers answers within two weeks, and approved teams get training on how to prepare their jobs.

Critics wonder whether the queue will become impossible. The lab says it will publish waiting times openly and adjust the share if demand is higher than expected.
