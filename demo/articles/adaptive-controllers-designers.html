Adaptive controllers: designers on making play work for everyone

Studios and hardware makers explain how they test with players who use many different ways to hold, press and move.

By Callum Reeve · November 4, 2026

A good controller feels invisible. For players with limited mobility, that is often not the case, which is why designers have started testing with a wide group of players from the start rather than at the end.

At the invented Quill Interactive studio, a standing panel of testers plays every build. Their feedback has led to remappable buttons, hold-to-toggle options and clear one-handed layouts.

Hardware makers are adding options too. Modular controllers let players swap thumbsticks, move buttons to the side or attach a switch, so that a single device can fit very different hands.

Designers say the improvements help everyone. "Whenever we make a game easier to hold, it also gets easier to enjoy," said accessibility lead Mina Torres.
