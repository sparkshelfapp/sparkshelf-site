The space station's next crew will test plants that grow without soil

A new experiment aims to find out how salad crops cope in microgravity using only mist and light.

By Dara Whitcombe · November 4, 2026

The next crew heading to the orbiting laboratory will carry a small garden. Its seedlings have no soil. Their roots hang in a fine mist of nutrient water, and a ring of lights replaces the sun.

The experiment, run by the invented Meridian Space Agency, asks a simple question: how much water and energy does a leafy crop need when there is no gravity to pull water downward?

The crew will measure growth every few days, photograph the roots, and taste the results. If all goes well, the leaves will be part of a meal in the final week of the mission.

Researchers hope the lessons will also help on Earth. "Growing food with very little water is useful in space and in a desert," said project scientist Dr. Leila Moreau.
