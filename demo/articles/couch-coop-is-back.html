Couch co-op is back: why local multiplayer is winning players over

Studios report that games built for one sofa and two controllers are among this year's quiet hits.

By Rosa Delacroix · November 6, 2026

After years of online lobbies and headsets, a surprising idea is returning: playing together in the same room. Several studios say their local multiplayer titles sold better than expected this season.

Developers point to the simplicity. There is no account to create, no waiting for a match, and no one to mute. You hand someone a controller, and you are playing in seconds.

What makes a good sofa game

Parents like it too. A shared screen makes it easy to see what children are playing and to join in, which several family surveys list as the main reason to buy.

The challenge for designers is to keep a game fun for two people with very different skill levels. The best titles let a beginner help in meaningful ways without feeling like a passenger.
