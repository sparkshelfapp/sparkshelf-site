Repairable by design: a new phone board swaps chips like building blocks

A startup's modular circuit board lets you replace the part that broke instead of the whole phone.

By Priya Natarajan · November 6, 2026

Open almost any modern phone and you will find one dense board where everything is soldered together. Kestrel Systems, a young company from the invented town of Alder Vale, wants to change that with a board built from small replaceable modules.

The prototype has separate carriers for the processor, the radios and the charging circuit. Each snaps into place with a spring connector and is held by two screws. A trained repairer can swap one in under ten minutes.

The company admits there is a price: the connectors take space, and the first units are a little thicker than a typical phone. Kestrel argues that a phone lasting seven years instead of three is worth the extra millimetre.

Repair groups welcomed the idea but asked for open documentation. Kestrel says it will publish the connector layout and a parts list when the first small batch ships next spring.
