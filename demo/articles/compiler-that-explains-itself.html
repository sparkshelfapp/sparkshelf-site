Lumen Labs open-sources a compiler that explains its own errors

Instead of a wall of codes, the new tool tells you what went wrong, where, and what to try next.

By Mara Ellison · November 6, 2026

Every programmer knows the feeling of a compiler message that reads like a riddle. Lumen Labs, a small studio from the invented coastal town of Port Harlow, says it has a cure: a compiler that writes its errors in plain sentences.

The tool, called Plainspeak, keeps the original error code for people who want it, but leads with a short explanation. A missing bracket becomes "this block is opened on line 12 and never closed", followed by one suggested fix you can accept with a keystroke.

What changes for beginners

Early testers were mostly students. In a four-week trial, the lab reports, first-year learners spent about a third less time stuck on syntax mistakes and finished more of their weekly exercises.

The project is released under a permissive licence and already has a handful of outside contributors translating the messages. "We wanted the first error a beginner sees to feel like a hint, not a verdict," said project lead Tomas Reyes.

Plainspeak works with a popular open scripting language today, with two more on the roadmap. The team says it will publish its message style guide so other tools can borrow the idea.
